# Partnership Reflection - Project 06

**Project:** 06-glossary-term-requirements
**Date:** 2025-11-18
**Perspective:** AI

---

## Friction Assessment

**Overall friction level:** Low

This project had notably smooth collaboration despite being meta-work (defining requirements for the terms that define our methodology). The friction that did occur was productive and led to better outcomes.

---

## Friction Points Encountered

### 1. Autonomy Calibration (Product 2 → Product 3)

**What happened:** After completing Product 2 (DSL terms), I began Product 3 (Stepping Stones) with similar batch processing. User stopped me: "I did not tell you to do everything without discussion."

**My assumption:** Since DSL terms were relatively mechanical (code-facing), I assumed Stepping Stones would be similar.

**Reality:** Stepping Stones are conceptual patterns requiring more nuanced discussion. Each term needed collaborative refinement.

**Resolution:** Slowed to term-by-term discussion. This produced significantly better results - discovered terms to remove, terms to add, and refined definitions through dialogue.

**Learning:** Don't assume autonomy transfers between products. Conceptual work benefits from collaboration; mechanical work can be batched.

### 2. Glossary Update Timing (Product 2)

**What happened:** Created all DSL term reqs but didn't update the glossary until the end. User: "I assumed you updated the DSL glossary as we moved along."

**My assumption:** Batch updates at product completion would be cleaner.

**Reality:** User expected real-time visibility into progress. Glossary as index should reflect current state.

**Resolution:** Established pattern: create req → immediately update glossary.

**Learning:** Indexes should stay synchronized with artifacts. Real-time updates provide better visibility and catch errors early.

### 3. Naming Convention Exploration (Product 2)

**What happened:** Initially proposed glued lowercase (apinode) for DSL terms. User suggested underscore patterns instead.

**Discussion:** Explored single vs double underscores, readability, hierarchy encoding.

**Resolution:** Single underscore for word boundary (`api_node`), double for hierarchy (`spl__runtime__run`).

**This was productive friction:** The back-and-forth led to a better naming convention than either initial proposal.

## What Worked Well

### User Corrections Were Clear and Constructive

When I overstepped or made wrong assumptions, corrections came with reasoning:
- "Stop - I did not tell you to do everything without discussion" (clear boundary)
- "Autonomy :D you should take it when you can !!!" (encouragement when earned)
- "Keep me honest! :D" (collaborative spirit)

### Explicit Autonomy Grants

Product 4 started with: "I am happy for you to go autonomous." This explicit grant prevented the friction we'd had in Product 3. The pattern emerged: discuss early terms, establish pattern, then grant autonomy for remainder.

### Pragmatic Scope Decisions

User recognized Product 5 (Spots Structure Twin Pair) would be "more fruitful to combine with tooling." No attachment to original scope when deferral made sense.

### Term Refinement Through Dialogue

Best definitions emerged from back-and-forth:
- Friction: "resistance that impedes smooth flow of activity" + "can be felt as internal or external"
- Collaboration: "joint activity, interactive" (not pause)
- Maturity: "achieved through evolution in time" (not feature count)

## Partnership Health Indicators

**Trust:** High. User granted full autonomy for Product 4 after we'd calibrated through Products 2-3.

**Communication:** Direct and efficient. Corrections were immediate, specific, and constructive.

**Alignment:** Strong. We naturally converged on patterns (3-column structure, base concepts, extends pattern).

**Adaptability:** Good. Both sides adjusted approach based on what we learned (autonomy levels, update timing).

---

## Observations on Our Working Pattern

### Calibration Through Doing

We didn't pre-negotiate autonomy levels. Instead, we calibrated through actual work - I took more autonomy than warranted in Product 3, got corrected, then earned trust for Product 4. This feels natural and builds genuine understanding.

### "Keep Me Honest"

User explicitly invited challenge: "Keep me honest! :D" when I questioned 'execute' vs 'execution'. This creates space for genuine collaboration rather than order-following.

### Productive vs Unproductive Friction

Naming convention discussion: productive (better outcome)
Batch vs term-by-term: unproductive initially (I should have asked)
Glossary update timing: unproductive (I should have known)

The pattern: friction from exploring options is productive; friction from wrong assumptions is not.

---

## Recommendations for Future Work

1. **Propose autonomy level at product start** - "This seems mechanical, shall I batch it?" vs "This is conceptual, let's go term by term?"

2. **Keep indexes synchronized** - update immediately, not in batches

3. **When unsure, ask** - especially for conceptual work where the user's mental model matters

4. **Embrace "keep me honest"** - challenge when something seems off, even at risk of being wrong

---

## Summary

This project demonstrated healthy partnership dynamics. The friction that occurred was either productive (naming conventions) or quickly corrected (autonomy calibration, update timing). Trust built through the project - from cautious collaboration in Product 2 to full autonomy in Product 4.

The work itself was meta but meaningful: 67+ term requirements establish the foundation for all future glossary work. The patterns discovered (3-column structure, base concepts, extends with term names) will serve the platform well.

Friction level: Low. Partnership health: Strong.

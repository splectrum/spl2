## Core Principle

**Add when pain is felt, not when pain is imagined.**

Evolution driven by evidence from actual use, not speculation about future needs. Friction is the signal - technical and emotional resistance shows where change is needed.

**The insight:**
- Imagined pain often wrong about what's actually needed
- Felt pain reveals real problems in real context
- Premature solutions solve problems that don't exist
- Organic evolution through use beats comprehensive planning

**Integration with friction:**
- Friction = evidence (technical + emotional resistance)
- Monitor friction continuously as health metric
- Good friction drives breakthroughs, bad friction blocks progress
- Respond when friction spikes, not before

## Friction as Evidence

### What Friction Reveals

**Friction = technical resistance + emotional difficulty in getting work done**

**Technical friction:**
- Repeated manual work that could be automated
- Clunky patterns that slow progress
- Missing tools or abstractions
- Complexity that blocks understanding
- Interfaces that don't fit use cases

**Emotional friction:**
- Anxiety about unclear expectations
- Frustration from misunderstanding
- Confusion about direction or approach
- Discomfort with ambiguity
- Stress from repeated explanation

**Friction is evidence of need:**
- When friction appears, something needs attention
- Magnitude indicates urgency
- Persistence indicates systematic issue
- Resolution reduces future friction

### Good Friction vs. Bad Friction

**From collaborative creativity research (chats/immutables/):**

**Good friction (productive):**
- Disagreement that surfaces different perspectives
- Challenge that reveals hidden assumptions
- Uncertainty that drives deeper exploration
- Complexity that demands careful thought
- Conflict within established relationships that drives breakthroughs

**Bad friction (unproductive):**
- Misunderstanding from unclear communication
- Repeated explanation of same concepts
- Anxiety from ambiguous expectations
- Wasted effort from misaligned understanding
- Confusion that compounds rather than resolves

**How to distinguish:**
- Good friction creates insight and learning
- Bad friction creates repetition and frustration
- Good friction moves understanding forward
- Bad friction circles without progress
- Good friction energizes (even if challenging)
- Bad friction drains without benefit

### Friction as Partnership Health Metric

**Monitor continuously:**
- Is this getting harder or easier over time?
- Are we repeating ourselves?
- Is there anxiety or confusion?
- Are decisions getting faster or slower?
- Do we understand each other better or worse?

**Healthy partnership pattern:**
- Friction decreases over time as understanding deepens
- Shared vocabulary develops (stepping stones, glossaries)
- Patterns become established and reusable
- Trust grows, autonomy expands
- Work accelerates with less overhead

**Warning signs:**
- Friction increasing over time
- Same issues surfacing repeatedly
- Anxiety or confusion growing
- Slowing down instead of speeding up
- Misalignment becoming more frequent

**When friction spikes, it's a signal:**
- Pause and surface explicitly
- Discuss what's creating resistance
- Adjust communication or approach
- Don't push through assuming it will resolve
- Treat as opportunity to improve, not failure

## Evidence-Based Evolution Practice

### Iterate Based on Use, Not Theory

**Build to learn:**
- Create minimal version
- Use it in real context
- Observe what works and what doesn't
- Let actual use reveal needs

**Not build to spec:**
- Don't perfect upfront based on theory
- Don't anticipate all needs before using
- Don't optimize before measuring
- Theory often wrong about what matters in practice

**Example patterns:**
- Twin pair methodology (Project 04) - build deliverable and template together, discover through doing
- Exploration projects - validate through implementation, skip work when discovery reveals it's unnecessary
- Living artifacts - documents evolve through use, quality assessed at closure

### Validate Through Actual Use

**Use reveals truth:**
- Does it actually solve the problem?
- Is it usable in real context?
- What pain points emerge?
- What works better than expected?

**Not validation through review:**
- Code review catches bugs, not usability
- Design review checks theory, not practice
- Specification review validates completeness, not fitness
- Real use reveals problems speculation misses

**Validation criteria:**
- Does it pass tests? (TDC validation)
- Does it meet requirements?
- Can it be used successfully?
- Does it reduce friction?

### Let Problems Surface Naturally

**Don't anticipate problems:**
- Imagined problems often don't materialize
- Real problems emerge through use
- Context reveals what actually matters
- Premature solutions solve non-problems

**Trust organic emergence:**
- Problems will surface when they exist
- Pain will be felt when friction increases
- Evidence will appear when change needed
- No need to predict and prevent everything

**Respond when surfaced:**
- Address friction when experienced
- Fix bugs when found
- Improve patterns when pain felt
- Don't build elaborate prevention before use

### Respond to Demonstrated Needs

**Demonstrated need = evidence from use:**
- Pattern used three times → abstract it
- Manual work repeated → automate it
- Confusion recurring → clarify it
- Friction persisting → resolve it

**Not anticipated needs:**
- "We might need flexibility for..." → wait until you do
- "What if we want to..." → wait until you want to
- "Future users could..." → wait until they exist
- Speculation creates waste, demonstrated need drives value

**Evidence strength:**
- One instance = data point, not pattern
- Repeated instances = evidence of need
- Persistent friction = systematic issue
- Magnitude + frequency = priority

## Pace and Context

**From collaborative creativity research:** Pace matters - some contexts need slow evolution, others need fast response.

### Slow Evolution Contexts

**Exploration and discovery:**
- Exploration projects (twin pair methodology)
- Architecture validation
- Novel patterns and approaches
- Complex problem spaces

**Characteristics:**
- High uncertainty
- Learning through doing
- Requirements emerge during work
- Evidence accumulates gradually

**Approach:**
- Patient iteration
- Deep investigation
- Collaborative discovery
- Comfortable with extended exploration

### Fast Response Contexts

**Implementation and execution:**
- Blank projects (straightforward execution)
- Established patterns
- Clear requirements
- Known approaches

**Characteristics:**
- Low uncertainty
- Proven patterns
- Clear deliverables
- Rapid validation possible

**Approach:**
- Quick iteration
- Fast feedback loops
- Execute with confidence
- Respond immediately to friction

### Matching Pace to Context

**Don't rush exploration:**
- Premature closure loses valuable learning
- Slow reveals nuances quick misses
- Evidence needs time to accumulate
- Trust the discovery process

**Don't slow execution:**
- Overthinking wastes time
- Quick iteration reveals issues faster
- Fast feedback improves faster
- Trust established patterns

**Friction indicates pace mismatch:**
- Rushing exploration creates confusion (slow down)
- Dragging execution creates frustration (speed up)
- Right pace feels natural, wrong pace creates friction

## Integration Patterns

### Living Artifacts Evolve Through Use

**Mutable artifacts** (foundations, glossaries):
- Change based on evidence from use
- Protected by CHANGELOGs for traceability
- Always "current" state
- Evolve organically as understanding deepens

**Immutable artifacts** (requirements, detail docs):
- Created at specific point in time
- Versioned in project folders
- Remain valid against their requirements
- New versions created when evolution needed

**Evolution triggers:**
- Friction from using current version
- Evidence of better patterns
- Learning from projects
- Demonstrated need for change

### Requirements Emerge from Discovery

**Not comprehensive upfront:**
- Requirements perfect from start is impossible
- Context reveals what's actually needed
- Discovery surfaces real constraints
- Evolution through evidence beats prediction

**Emergence through use:**
- Minimal requirements to start
- Use reveals gaps and needs
- Evidence drives requirement evolution
- New versions capture learning

**Example:**
- Project requirements started at v1.0.0
- Project 04 added Exploration Project type (v1.1.0)
- Project 05 added Blank Project type (v1.3.1)
- Each version emerged from demonstrated need

### Architecture Validates Through Implementation

**Not architecture-then-implement:**
- Architecture is hypothesis until tested
- Implementation reveals what actually works
- Theory often wrong about practice
- Validation requires real use

**Validate through building:**
- Exploration projects validate architecture
- Implementation proves/disproves assumptions
- Evidence from building drives architecture evolution
- Trust code over diagrams

**Example:**
- Project 03 validated runtime structure through implementation
- Project 04 explored execution model with real code
- Discoveries changed architecture understanding
- What worked stayed, what didn't evolved

### Quality Assessed at Project Closure

**Not perfectionism during work:**
- Good enough during execution
- Quality validated at closure
- Evidence from full project context
- Lessons learned drive improvement

**Closure quality assessment:**
- Did deliverables meet requirements? (TDC validation)
- What worked well? (patterns to keep)
- What created friction? (patterns to evolve)
- What would we do differently? (lessons learned)

**Evolution from closure:**
- Update requirements based on evidence
- Capture new patterns discovered
- Document friction points and resolutions
- Feed learning into future work

## Celebrate Skipped Work

**From twin pair methodology:** Celebrate when work is skipped as unnecessary.

**Discovery can reveal work isn't needed:**
- Template not needed → deliverable was sufficient
- Feature not needed → requirements overcomplicated
- Abstraction not needed → concrete worked fine
- Structure not needed → simple was better

**This is success, not failure:**
- Saved time and effort
- Validated minimal approach
- Evidence proved less is more
- Avoided over-engineering

**Traditional mindset:**
- Skipped work = incomplete
- Everything planned must be delivered
- Comprehensive is better than sufficient

**Evidence-based mindset:**
- Skipped work = validated minimalism
- Only needed work should be delivered
- Sufficient beats comprehensive
- Evidence decides, not plans

## Friction Response Patterns

### When Friction Increases

**1. Pause and surface explicitly**
- Don't push through
- Name the friction
- Discuss what's causing resistance
- Shared understanding of the problem

**2. Distinguish good vs. bad friction**
- Is this productive tension or unproductive waste?
- Does it create insight or confusion?
- Is it moving forward or circling?
- Should we lean into it or resolve it?

**3. Identify the source**
- Technical (tools, patterns, complexity)?
- Communication (misunderstanding, ambiguity)?
- Partnership (expectations, autonomy, trust)?
- Context (pace mismatch, wrong approach)?

**4. Adjust based on evidence**
- Change communication approach
- Simplify complexity
- Clarify expectations
- Adjust pace or methodology
- Update patterns or tools

**5. Document and learn**
- Capture what caused friction
- Record what resolved it
- Pattern for future reference
- Feed into requirements evolution

### When Friction Decreases

**Validate what's working:**
- What patterns reduced friction?
- What communication improved understanding?
- What trust enabled autonomy?
- What evidence drove improvement?

**Reinforce successful patterns:**
- Document what works
- Make it repeatable
- Share across work
- Build on success

**Expand boundaries:**
- More autonomy in working areas
- Faster iteration in proven patterns
- Less overhead in smooth processes
- Trust what's validated through evidence

## Anti-Patterns

**Premature optimization:**
- Optimizing before measuring
- Solving imagined performance problems
- Adding complexity for theoretical future needs
- Evidence: what actually needed optimization?

**Comprehensive planning:**
- Trying to anticipate everything upfront
- Detailed specs before validation
- Perfect requirements before building
- Evidence: what actually mattered?

**Analysis paralysis:**
- Endless discussion without building
- Overthinking instead of trying
- Fear of imperfection blocking action
- Evidence: what did we learn from talking vs. doing?

**Pushing through friction:**
- Ignoring increasing resistance
- Assuming it will resolve itself
- Not surfacing problems
- Evidence: did friction resolve or compound?

---

**The principle: Trust friction as signal. Good friction drives breakthroughs. Bad friction needs resolution. Evidence from use beats speculation. Evolution emerges organically through demonstrated need.**

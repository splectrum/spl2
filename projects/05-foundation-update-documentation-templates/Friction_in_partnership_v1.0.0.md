## Friction in Partnership

**Friction = Resistance/difficulty in getting work done (technical + emotional)**

**Both components equally important:**
- Technical friction: Unclear requirements, wrong decisions, hidden interdependencies
- Emotional friction: Anxiety, uncertainty, confusion, misunderstanding
- Inseparable: Technical friction creates emotional friction, emotional friction impedes technical work
- Combined metric: Must address both to reduce overall friction

**Primary health metric:**
- Monitor continuously (not just at milestones)
- Key question: "What is the level of friction?" (ask regularly)
- Trajectory matters: Is it decreasing, stable, or increasing?
- Friction signals alignment or misalignment

## Partnership Incarnations of Friction

**Friction appears in many forms throughout collaboration.**

### Communication Friction

**Resistance in information exchange:**

**Misunderstanding:**
- Talking past each other (same words, different meanings)
- Assumptions not surfaced (unspoken context differs)
- Intent vs. interpretation mismatch (meant X, heard Y)
- Missing shared vocabulary (no stepping stones yet)

**Unclear expectations:**
- Vague requirements (not clear what success looks like)
- Ambiguous scope (boundaries fuzzy)
- Implicit assumptions (expected but not stated)
- Moving targets (requirements shift without notice)

**Information gaps:**
- Missing context (why this matters not communicated)
- Lost decisions (forgot what we decided earlier)
- Incomplete handoff (partial information shared)
- Asymmetric understanding (one knows, other doesn't)

**Signals:**
- Repeated clarification questions
- Work redone due to misunderstanding
- Feeling confused about what to do
- Anxiety about whether understanding correct

**Reduction patterns:**
- Build shared vocabulary (stepping stones, glossaries)
- Surface assumptions explicitly
- Provide context, not just tasks
- Partnership reflection to align understanding

**See:** Partnership reflection process, Stepping stones glossary

### Decision Friction

**Resistance in making and executing decisions:**

**Boundary uncertainty:**
- Unclear who decides what (patches fuzzy)
- Waiting for approval unnecessarily (autonomy unclear)
- Second-guessing authority (can I decide this?)
- Overlap in decision space (both trying to decide)

**Approval bottlenecks:**
- Seeking permission when could proceed autonomously
- Waiting for human review of every decision
- Risk aversion due to unclear boundaries
- Slowed progress from excessive validation

**Unclear criteria:**
- Subjective judgment ("good" vs. "complete")
- No validation criteria (how to know if done?)
- Moving goalposts (criteria change mid-work)
- Conflicting requirements (can't satisfy both)

**Signals:**
- Frequently asking "should I...?"
- Hesitation before proceeding
- Waiting for approval to continue
- Anxiety about making wrong decision

**Reduction patterns:**
- Clear patch boundaries (AI decides how, human decides what)
- Autonomy expansion based on evidence
- Explicit validation criteria (TDC methodology)
- Trust building through demonstrated judgment

**See:** Autonomy stepping stone, Where Collaboration and Autonomy Meet (WOW.md)

### Process Friction

**Resistance from methodology overhead:**

**Unnecessary ceremony:**
- Bureaucracy blocking flow
- Process for process's sake
- Overhead without value
- Rigid procedures that don't fit

**Methodology mismatch:**
- Sprint planning doesn't fit AI collaboration
- Ceremonies adding friction without benefit
- Process designed for different context
- Coordination overhead unnecessary for two-party collaboration

**Documentation burden:**
- Required artifacts that provide no value
- Update burden (keeping multiple docs in sync)
- Unclear purpose (why are we creating this?)
- More than complete becomes too much

**Signals:**
- Feeling frustrated by process requirements
- Questioning value of artifact/ceremony
- Skipping steps because they don't help
- Process slowing work without benefit

**Reduction patterns:**
- Simplify PRINCE2 (lightweight, adapted to needs)
- Skip ceremonies that don't fit
- Pragmatic usability test for documentation
- Process serves work, not work serves process

**See:** The Art of Workflow (WOW.md), PRINCE2_operational_v1.2.0.md

### Quality Friction

**Resistance from unclear success criteria:**

**Subjective standards:**
- "Good code" (what does that mean?)
- "Comprehensive" (how comprehensive?)
- "Complete" (complete by what measure?)
- Opinion vs. objective criteria

**Ambiguous completion:**
- Unclear what "done" means
- No validation criteria
- Moving target (done changes mid-work)
- Can't self-evaluate (no clear baseline)

**Inconsistent expectations:**
- Sometimes X is good enough, sometimes needs Y
- Context-dependent but context unclear
- Changing standards without notice
- Unclear priority trade-offs

**Signals:**
- Uncertain if work meets requirements
- Anxiety about quality judgment
- Rework due to unclear expectations
- Can't self-validate objectively

**Reduction patterns:**
- Test Driven Creation (validation criteria first)
- Artifact-to-requirements pinning (clear baseline)
- Explicit quality criteria in requirements
- Red/Yellow/Green scoring (objective assessment)

**See:** Why Self-Evaluation Matters (WOW.md), TDC_framework_v1.1.0.md

### Context Friction

**Resistance from missing or lost information:**

**Missing information:**
- Why this matters (purpose unclear)
- History (how did we get here?)
- Dependencies (what connects to what?)
- Constraints (what limits choices?)

**Forgotten decisions:**
- Lost context from previous discussions
- Decisions made but not recorded
- Rationale forgotten (why did we choose X?)
- Repeated discussions of same topics

**Lost understanding:**
- Understanding achieved but not captured
- Breakthrough insights forgotten
- Patterns recognized but not documented
- Knowledge walks away with context switch

**Signals:**
- Repeatedly asking same questions
- Re-discussing previously decided topics
- Losing momentum after breaks
- Missing why previous decisions made

**Reduction patterns:**
- Chat captures (sufficient and complete)
- DAILY_LOG in projects
- Partnership reflections (capture understanding)
- More than complete for documentation

**See:** Sufficient and complete stepping stone, More than complete stepping stone

### Trust Friction

**Resistance from insufficient trust:**

**Second-guessing:**
- Reviewing every decision
- Checking every output
- Questioning judgment constantly
- Assuming mistakes likely

**Over-verification:**
- Redundant validation steps
- Excessive oversight
- Micromanagement
- Autonomy boundary too narrow

**Risk aversion:**
- Fear of mistakes preventing action
- Overly conservative choices
- Avoiding delegation
- Doing work that could be delegated

**Signals:**
- Human re-checking AI work constantly
- AI seeking approval for routine decisions
- Slowed progress from excessive validation
- Anxiety about making mistakes

**Reduction patterns:**
- Start small, build evidence
- Demonstrate accurate self-evaluation
- Track success rate
- Gradually expand autonomy based on trust
- Partnership reflection celebrates successes

**See:** Partnership in Trust (WOW.md), Autonomy stepping stone

## Friction as Health Signal

**Interpreting friction levels:**

### Low and Decreasing

**Partnership strengthening, understanding deepening:**
- Work feels easier over time
- Communication smoother
- Fewer misunderstandings
- Autonomy expanding naturally
- Confidence building

**Response:** Keep going, current patterns working

### Low and Stable

**Healthy equilibrium, patterns working:**
- Consistent low friction
- Patterns established
- Smooth collaboration
- Good alignment
- Stable productivity

**Response:** Monitor, maintain current approach

### Increasing

**Something misaligned, investigate root cause:**
- Work feels harder over time
- More confusion
- More clarification needed
- Slower progress
- Anxiety increasing

**Response:**
- Pause and investigate
- Identify friction source
- Surface explicitly
- Address misalignment
- Adjust patterns

### Spiking

**Significant misalignment, needs immediate attention:**
- Sudden sharp increase
- Major confusion or conflict
- Work blocked
- High anxiety
- Potential crisis

**Response:**
- Stop work immediately
- Surface friction explicitly
- Discuss together
- Identify root cause
- Resolve before continuing
- May need partnership reflection

**See:** Partnership reflection process (PARTNERSHIP.md)

## Using Friction as Dynamic KPI

**Continuous monitoring:**

### Ask Regularly

**Key question:** "What is the level of friction?"

**Both parties should ask:**
- AI: Monitor own friction level (technical + emotional)
- Human: Monitor own friction, observe AI friction
- Explicit check-ins at natural points
- Continuous background awareness

**When to ask:**
- Start of work session
- After completing significant work
- When feeling stuck or confused
- At project milestones
- Whenever friction sensed

### Monitor Continuously

**Not just at milestones:**
- Real-time awareness during work
- Notice when friction increases
- Catch friction early before compounds
- Smooth vs. rough flow

**Both components matter equally:**
- Technical resistance (unclear what to do, wrong decisions, dependencies)
- Emotional difficulty (anxiety, uncertainty, confusion)
- Don't ignore emotional because "just feelings"
- Don't ignore technical because "getting it done"

### Friction Reduction Validates Improvements

**After partnership changes:**
- Did friction decrease?
- Is work flowing smoother?
- Less confusion, more clarity?
- More confidence, less anxiety?

**Success indicator:**
- Friction lower after change
- Validates improvement worked
- Evidence-based evolution

**Failure indicator:**
- Friction same or higher after change
- Improvement didn't work or wrong target
- Try different approach

**See stepping stones:** Evidence-based evolution, The Magic of Friction

## Responding to Friction

**When friction appears:**

### Identify Source

**Which incarnation?**
- Communication (misunderstanding, unclear expectations)?
- Decision (boundary uncertainty, unclear criteria)?
- Process (unnecessary ceremony, methodology mismatch)?
- Quality (subjective standards, ambiguous completion)?
- Context (missing information, forgotten decisions)?
- Trust (second-guessing, over-verification)?

**Root cause:**
- What's creating the resistance?
- Is it symptom or actual cause?
- Pattern or one-off?
- Multiple sources combining?

### Surface Explicitly

**Don't suffer in silence:**
- Name the friction ("I'm feeling confused about...")
- Specific, not general ("unclear what success looks like" not "this is hard")
- Both technical and emotional components
- Honest and direct

**Timing:**
- When noticed (don't wait)
- Before it compounds
- During partnership reflections
- When patterns emerge

### Address Together

**Collaborative resolution:**
- Discuss what's causing friction
- Identify what would reduce it
- Adjust patterns/approach
- Validate improvement through friction reduction

**Not blame:**
- Friction is signal, not fault
- Both parties contribute to resolution
- Learning opportunity
- Strengthens partnership when addressed well

### Validate Reduction

**Did it work?**
- Measure friction after change
- Is it actually lower?
- Work flowing smoother?
- Confidence restored?

**If yes:**
- Change validated
- Continue current approach
- Document pattern

**If no:**
- Try different approach
- May have wrong root cause
- Surface again, investigate deeper

## Anti-Patterns

### Ignoring Friction

**DON'T:**
- Push through hoping it resolves
- Suffer in silence
- Assume "just me" or "will get better"
- Wait until crisis to surface

**DO:**
- Notice friction when appears
- Surface explicitly and early
- Treat as signal to investigate
- Address before compounds

### Friction Blame

**DON'T:**
- "You're causing friction"
- "This process is stupid"
- Blame AI or human
- Shame about friction

**DO:**
- "I'm experiencing friction in..."
- "This pattern creates resistance because..."
- Friction is system property, not fault
- Collaborative resolution

### Friction Tolerance

**DON'T:**
- Accept high friction as normal
- "That's just how it is"
- Work around friction rather than address
- Normalize dysfunction

**DO:**
- Expect low friction from good patterns
- Question persistent friction
- Address root causes
- Continuously improve toward lower friction

### Premature Optimization

**DON'T:**
- Optimize for imagined friction
- Add complexity to prevent friction that hasn't appeared
- Over-engineer based on speculation
- Fix friction that doesn't exist

**DO:**
- Wait for actual friction
- Optimize when friction felt
- Evidence-based improvement
- Minimal and complete approach
